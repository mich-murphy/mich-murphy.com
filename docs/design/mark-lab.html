Building the favicon set

  • SVG first. <link rel="icon" href="/favicon.svg" type="image/svg+xml"> with a @media (prefers-color-scheme:dark) block inside the SVG, so the icon follows the reader's OS the way the site does. It's the same pixel map as the header, at about 600 bytes.
  • PNG fallback. A 32×32 favicon.png in the dark palette for browsers without SVG icons, plus a 180×180 apple-touch-icon.png: the mark at 8× on a #0f1214 plate with 10px of padding. Hugo can make both from one source PNG with images.Process.
  • Plates. Stamp, Content hash, Window and Link light carry their own square. Shadow stamp, Card, Graph and Box m are open shapes, so their favicons sit on a bg plate. Without one, bone lines vanish on a light tab strip. The tabs in the cards above show the plated versions.
  • Grid. The marks are 16 cells wide, so they're crisp at 16, 32 and 48 device pixels. In the header, 16px and 24px land on whole device pixels on a 2× screen. 20px, the size in the handover, gives 2.5 pixels per cell, so stems alternate between 2 and 3 pixels. Switch the header size to compare.

Review

rev 6 design · measured in Chrome 153

Three passes went into this. The first was a readability and accessibility audit, with contrast computed for every token pair in both modes and 0xProto's metrics read from the font files. The second was a fresh-eyes critique of cohesion and completeness, made from screenshots of the lab. The third was my own read, with every number checked against the posts. The IDs below are for replying: pick the ones you want, and the line at the bottom collects them.

Readability

Yes, with a few fixes.

The size, measure and line height are right for an all-mono long read. Three light-mode pairs fail contrast, the contents list has a keyboard bug, and h3 relies on colour alone.

Cohesion and completeness

One system, a few drifts.

The frame, palette and square hold it together. The drift: title bars on pathless one-liners, dither on everything, and uppercase mangling data. Still undesigned: the end of a post, tags, and what /posts/ is.

Engineering cues

Real, but three would lie on launch day.

The colophon is the strongest idea. changed, atom.xml and 0 B js aren't true yet. Once they are, there's room for one or two more cues that carry real data.

Does it promote readability?

Mostly, yes. 0xProto has a tall x-height (0.55em), so 15px 0xProto reads about as large as 16px SF Pro. At 74 characters, the column sits at the top of the comfortable range, and line-height 1.7 is generous. Every text pair passes AA except one. The problems are in the details: hierarchy that relies only on colour, one light-mode syntax colour, and interaction states nobody has drawn yet. One of them is a real bug.

  1. R1

    Keyboard and screen-reader users can't open the contents listbug

    The hover box is hidden with visibility:hidden, which makes its links unfocusable. The ticks are tabindex=-1, so :focus-within can never fire. In Chrome's accessibility tree the “Contents” nav is empty. Also, the box is centred with translateY(-50%), so at the top of a post it covers the header.

    Fix Hide with opacity:0; pointer-events:none instead, and show on .rail:is(:hover,:focus-within). Anchor the box at top:0.

  2. R2

    The light-mode focus ring is nearly invisiblefails AA

    #e7a15a on #f5f4f0 is 1.98:1, and on code backgrounds it's 1.79:1. Non-text needs 3:1. It's fine in dark mode (8.63:1).

    Fix Add a --focus token: acc in dark mode, fg in light. Apply it on .site :focus-visible so pre and summary get it too.

  3. R3

    Light-mode code comments fail contrastfails AA

    com #6f7a82 on #ebe9e3 is 3.62:1 at 13.5px. Light-mode strings pass by only 0.07 (4.57:1).

    Fix Change light com to #5f6a72 (4.56:1). It stays the same cool grey.

  4. R4

    h3 looks like a one-line paragraphoption

    h3 has the same size and weight as body text. Only the step from 74% to 100% brightness sets it apart: ΔL* 19.6 in dark, 27.3 in light. In forced-colours mode that step disappears. 7 posts use 22 h3s.

    Option .prose h3{font-weight:700}, which loads the Bold file on those 7 posts only. The zero-byte alternative is font-size:1.1em plus more space above.

  5. R5

    Dark-mode body text is dim, and meta text is barely dimmeroption

    The 74% mix (#aeaca4) passes WCAG at 8.27:1, but it scores APCA Lc 57, below the Lc 75 recommended for body text. Meta text in mute is only 1.16:1 away from it, so the two tiers blur. Light mode scores Lc 83 and is fine.

    Option Use an 85% mix in dark mode only (#c6c3b9, Lc 70) and keep 74% in light. Take it together with R4, because it shrinks the h3 step. Dropping -webkit-font-smoothing:antialiased also stops macOS thinning light-on-dark text.

  6. R6

    Code gets cut off with no sign that it scrollsoption

    At 13.5px a character is 8.37px wide. The desktop window fits 77 characters, and 40 lines in 27 of the 69 blocks run past it. A 390px phone fits 38, so 28% of all code lines are cut. macOS hides scrollbars until you scroll, so lines just end mid-token.

    Option A CSS-only scroll shadow on .win pre (background-attachment: local, scroll), and tabindex="0" on <pre> in the render hook so keyboards can scroll it. Keep scrolling rather than wrapping: with no line numbers, a wrapped line would look like two lines.

  7. R7

    The contents-rail ticks are too faintfails AA

    Ticks in edge are 1.93:1 in dark and 1.97:1 in light, below the 3:1 non-text minimum. They're the only sign that the rail exists.

    Fix Draw ticks in mute (7.1 and 5.3:1) and the current tick in fg at 24px, and make hover go to fg.

  8. R8

    Windows High Contrast drops bullets, ticks and the current nav itemfix

    Forced colours strip backgrounds, so the square bullets and ticks, which are backgrounds, vanish. The inverted current item and the inline-code chip vanish too.

    Fix About 150 bytes: @media (forced-colors:active) gives bullets and ticks CanvasText with forced-color-adjust:none, and underlines [aria-current].

  9. R9

    Small fixes: colophon label, tap targets, inline codefix

    At 320px the colophon's path label wraps to two lines and overlaps “filed” by 4px. The 50-character slugs will wrap at 390px too. Header nav links are 20px tall, and uppercase turns 27256ac into 27256AC and atom.xml into ATOM.XML. The inline-code chip is 1.04:1 against the page, so code in running text is marked only by its brightness.

    Fix Under 600px, .colo-path .frame{padding-top:38px}. Extend the nav and footer hit areas with an ::after at inset:-12px -6px. Add text-transform:none on the hash and the feed link. Set .prose :not(pre)>code{color:var(--fg)}.

Keep: 15px / 1.7 / 74ch in 0xProto; 14.5px on phones; links underlined in the text colour with the hover inversion; square bullets and numbers in fg; h2 at 1.25em; scrolling code rather than wrapping it. Nothing scrolls sideways at 320px, and only code and figures scroll inside their frames.

Does it hold together?

Yes. It reads as one system. The 2px fg frame, the tuned palette and the square hold it together: the 7px bullets and the contents marker echo the stamp's pixels. Some differences are healthy. Title bars frame files and legends label the site's own boxes. Lowercase keys like filed are data, while Title Case labels like Posts are names. The drift is in a few specific places.

  1. C1

    Title bars with nothing to sayoption

    44 of the 69 blocks have no file path, so they get a full bone bar that just says “Bash”. 26 of the 40 Bash blocks are a single line, so the bar is as tall as the code. On a post page the bars are the loudest thing, and the eye jumps from bar to bar past the h2s.

    Option Keep the title bar only when a block has a path or a figure title. Pathless blocks take B, the language as a label cut into the border (the box-label rule the site already has), or C, a plain wash with a 2px left rule. Then the rule is simple: a title bar means a file.

  2. C2

    The dither stops meaning anything on long postsoption

    backup-solutions-nixos will carry 11 dithered boxes: 8 code windows, 2 notes and the colophon. On the home page the dither marks the two index cards. On a post it becomes texture.

    Option a: index cards only (Posts, Projects, Tags and the social card). b: one per page (the Posts card on home, the colophon on posts). c: leave it as it is. You added the dither to code on purpose in rev 6, so this is only a question.

  3. C3

    Uppercase changes the data, and settles the Title Case questionoption

    The footer renders BUILD 27256AC · ATOM.XML while the colophon says 27256ac. A hash and a filename shouldn't change case. The nav items are paths too.

    Option Keep uppercase for the name only. Set the nav in lowercase as written (posts tags about atom.xml), and leave the footer's data untransformed. This answers the open header question from the handover without moving to Title Case.

  4. C4

    Inversion means three thingsoption

    An fg fill marks title bars, the current page and link hover, so a hovered nav item looks like the current one.

    Option Mark the current item with the 7px square the Contents box already uses (■ posts), and keep inversion for hover and title bars.

  5. C5

    Eight type sizes where five would do, and a rule that overshootsfix

    The sizes in use are 11, 12, 13, 13.5, 14, 15, 18.75 and 24px. The 11px footer against the 12px header is drift, not a choice. The post-header rule spans the 772px wrap, but everything under it stops at 74ch, about 84px short.

    Fix Collapse to 12 / 13.5 / 15 / 19 / 24, with the footer at 12. Set .ph{max-width:74ch}.

  6. C6

    The accent has no single meaningoption

    Orange is the stamp's corner, the code strings, the selection and the focus ring. The handover specs acct for the commit hash, but the path colophon draws it in fg, so today strings are the only orange text.

    Option With the Graph mark, orange means HEAD. Draw the colophon's commit and the footer's build hash in acct too, and the mark, the colophon and the footer all agree. Strings stay orange as syntax. Skip this if you keep a mark whose orange is decorative.

Is it complete?

The design covers home, the post and the social card thoroughly. These are the gaps. The first group changes how the templates are shaped, so decide them before the build. The second can be settled while building.

Decide before the build
  • What /posts/ is. Home already lists all 17 posts, and the nav marks “posts” as current on home. Either home is the index, or home shows the latest 6 and /posts/ holds the full card with a rule at each year.
  • The end of a post. Only the colophon sits there now. Older and Newer rows under it, styled like the Posts card without the dither, would keep readers moving.
  • Tags. 20 of the 26 tags have one post. Use a counted card for the six tags with two or more posts (nixos 11, homelab 10, and four with 2), followed by the singletons as an inline list. Term pages get a #nixos · 11 label.
  • Contents-rail threshold. The systemd post has one h2, which gives one lonely tick. Show the rail at 3 headings or more.
  • Heading anchors. A mute # at −2ch on :hover and :focus-within, so readers can copy a section link without any JS.
  • Long titles on the social card. Test a 60-character title with images.Text: three lines at most, stepping the size down as needed.
Settle while building
  • 404 as a framed box above the Posts card.
  • A skip link as an inverted chip on focus.
  • Two theme-color metas with media.
  • The favicon set from the marks section.
  • Print: no dither, rail or nav; pre{white-space:pre-wrap}; external URLs printed after links.
  • scrollbar-color:var(--edge) var(--code) on code.
  • :visited, <details>, and images inside the figure frame, for when a post first needs them.
  • Mermaid: its min-width:600px cuts the figure on phones, and 11px labels shrink to 9.6px. Let it scroll at natural size like code.

Does it give subtle engineering cues?

Yes, and it's the best idea in the design: the colophon treats the post as a file with a path, a commit and a history. Most cues show real data. Three would be untrue on launch day, so fix those before adding more.

  5. E5

  6. E6

    The rail as a scalelater

    Space the ticks in proportion to each section's word count, so the rail becomes a true minimap of the post. This needs a per-tick CSS variable and some template work.

Too much: diff stats and commit heatmaps, $ prompts and ~/ breadcrumbs, /nix/store hashes, “deployed 3h ago”. Also a Hugo and nixpkgs version line, unless CI builds through the flake: CI runs Hugo 0.166.0 and the flake pins 0.152.2, so the line would be wrong.

What to keep, and what I'd push for

Keep
  • The restraint: one face, one frame, and headings set apart by contrast. Orange stays rare, and light mode is as good as dark.
  • The colophon as a file, the idea that justifies the whole look.
  • Counted border labels, and the zero-JS rail once R1 is fixed.
Push for
  • R1–R3 and R7: the keyboard bug and the three contrast failures. These are fixes, not taste.
  • C1: two tiers of code block. It's the biggest visual change for the least work.
  • E1: no cue that lies on launch day.
  • For the mark, Graph. It keeps the M, gives the orange a meaning, and pairs with C6. Content hash is the runner-up and the purest idea.
